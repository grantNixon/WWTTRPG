Getting Started

Similar to Pathfinder taking D&D's 3.5E and making their own version of the game, Outer West's system is based on the 5th edition of Dungeons and Dragons. Outer West takes D&D's 5E and tweaks the rules to make a unique ruleset with a focus on build variety

Our goal with Outer West and it's systems is to give players variety each turn. What we noticed as developers is that with some "D20" based rpg's, you're really limited to a couple things you can do in your turn, typically only having an action and bonus action. With OW, instead of certain actions being grouped into an action or bonus action, characters use a bank of action points and a movement bank to utilize at their disposal.

A Joint Story

Like similiar RPGs, playing Outer West is a joint story created by the game master or "GM" and the players. The GM narrates the story and the players play the protagonists in that story. This story is often called a campaign and is broken down in blocks of playtime called "sessions". Let's go through an example session below to give you an expample of how the game is played

Setting the Scene

GM: You all start in a saloon, partners. The drinks are flowing and the cards are turning. Your posses' bounty target; "Big Boy Billy", is playing poker at the other end of the saloon. What do you all want to do?

"I want to go to the bar and get a drink but I want to try to get a discount"
GM: roll a Barter check

"I want to join the pianist in playing a song"
GM: roll a Performance check

"I want to stare down Bart and try to scare him"
GM: roll an Intimidation check

A Fight!

GM: Your posse confronts Billy and his fledgling gang and a fight breaks out! Everyone else works their way out of the saloon in a chaotic stampede as the posse gets ready to fight. Everyone roll initiative.

Every fight starts with initiative — another word for turn order.

Combat Basics

After initiative, the first person can make a move. Let's go over the basics:

AP
Action Points are the bank of points used to perform actions. You regain 3 AP at the start of your turn.

MB
Movement Bank is the amount of movement (in feet) you can make in a turn. Each square or hex equals 5ft. So an MB of 25ft lets you move 5 squares.

Damage
You can take damage from hits or effects like poison. Your Evasion Chance (EC) determines if an attack hits — the enemy must roll your EC or higher. For effects, you make a saving throw like Gum.S(10), rolling your Gumption bonus to beat the target number.

Time to Jump In, Partners

All this makes up the very basics of how the game is played. It's a mix of roleplaying and combat scenarios over the course of the sessions until the campaign, whether short or long, is over. So grab some friends, test out some sessions, and remember: it's a collaboration, not a competition.
